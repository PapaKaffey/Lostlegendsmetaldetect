Before the search

Frequently asked questions

What to do after you lose something, how the reward works, and what to expect when I come out.

Text Alex Request a search
For the fastest reply, text the item, an address or GPS pin, and a photo of the search area.
What should I do if I just lost a ring?

Mark the last spot where you remember wearing it. For the fastest reply, text me what you lost, an address or GPS pin, and a photo of the search area. Tell me if the area is about to be mowed or plowed.

What does a lost-ring or jewelry search cost?

You set the reward after I return your item. No find, no fee. The only exception is a travel fee we agree on before a remote trip.

What happens if you don’t find my item?

You’ll get a report of where I searched and practical next steps, such as another spot to check or whether a return visit makes sense. You pay nothing unless we agreed on a travel fee for a remote trip in advance.

Is the $75 property pin service a land survey?

I use a metal detector to identify existing markers only. This is not a land survey and does not establish, verify, or certify property boundaries. For boundary questions, contact a licensed land surveyor.

Should I rent a metal detector or hire you?

A rental can work if you know the spot and have time to learn the controls. In ground full of nails or pull tabs, it takes practice to pick out a ring. I bring my own equipment, search in overlapping passes, and check signals from more than one direction.

Can you search in water or snow?

Yes. I use waterproof equipment for shallow-water searches when conditions allow. Tell me how deep the water was, what the bottom is like, and where you went in. For a snow loss, mark the spot and tell me where any shoveled or plowed snow ended up.

How quickly can you respond?

I check texts frequently. Depending on my schedule and the drive, I may be able to come out the same day or the next, including evenings and weekends. I’ll confirm a time with you before I head out.

What are the chances of finding my item?

The best lead is a specific spot where you know you had it. The chances depend on how much time has passed, whether the item is still there, and how much ground or debris there is to search. Tell me what’s changed since the loss and I’ll talk through what’s realistic.

How should I prepare the search area?

Mark the likely spot with a landmark or photo and note the route you took. If possible, hold off on mowing, raking, shoveling, or moving soil. Tell me where you’ve already looked and arrange permission if someone else owns or manages the property.

How do you protect my yard and privacy?

I pinpoint the target before I dig. In a lawn, I cut a clean plug, refill the hole neatly, and replace the turf whenever conditions allow. I ask before sharing photos or details that identify you or your property.

Where do you take jobs?

I’m based in Plattsmouth. My core area is the Omaha metro, Council Bluffs, Cass County, and Nebraska City. For other parts of Nebraska or Iowa, text your nearest town so I can confirm the trip.

Can you search on someone else’s property?

We’ll need permission from the property owner or whoever manages the site before I start. If it’s a park, venue, job site, or rental property, send me the location and let me know who can grant access.

Can you find keys, watches, or other jewelry?

Yes. Tell me what it’s made of and whether there are other metal pieces attached. A picture helps, especially for small earrings, pendants, and keys with a plastic fob.

How much do property pin locating and cleanup cost?

Property pin locating is $75 per detectable marker. Junk metal cleanup is $89 per hour. I’ll confirm the scope and any travel charge before scheduling.
